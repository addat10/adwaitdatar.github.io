I am a postdoctoral researcher at the [Institute for Data Science Foundations](https://www.tuhh.de/dsf/homepage) at [TU Hamburg](https://www.tuhh.de/), working with Nihat Ay. My current research concerns natural-gradient optimization and Wasserstein-geometric approaches to divergence and regularization, with applications to learning and optimal control.

I am also drawing on tools from control theory to investigate how policy architectures affect the performance and robustness of embodied agents, with the longer-term goal of applying these ideas to robotic systems.

I completed my PhD at TU Hamburg under the supervision of Herbert Werner. I studied distributed control of multi-vehicle systems, using integral quadratic constraints to analyze stability and performance, and investigating cooperative source-seeking algorithms through experiments with groups of quadrotors. My approach involved moving between mathematical analysis, simulation, and experiments.

## Research Interests

Information Geometry · Optimization · Control Theory · Learning and Dynamical Systems

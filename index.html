About

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute for Data Science Foundations at TU Hamburg, working with Nihat Ay. My current research concerns natural-gradient optimization and Wasserstein-geometric approaches to divergence and regularization, with applications to learning and optimal control.

I completed my PhD at TU Hamburg under the supervision of Herbert Werner. I studied distributed control of multi-vehicle systems, using integral quadratic constraints to analyze stability and performance, and investigating cooperative source-seeking algorithms through experiments with groups of quadrotors. My approach involved moving between mathematical analysis, simulation, and experiments.

Drone experiments

02

Source seeking with Crazyflie quadrotors

During my PhD, I worked on indoor experiments in which a group of quadrotors cooperatively searches for the minimum of a scalar field. Local gradient and Hessian estimates are used to implement continuous versions of steepest descent and Newton-type methods with momentum.

This work with Peter Paulsen and Herbert Werner is presented in Flocking Towards the Source: Indoor Experiments with Quadrotors (ECC 2020).
